Getting views without paying

How Shorts views are earned

YouTube shows each new Short to a small test audience, then widens it if those viewers keep watching. The score it uses is viewed vs. swiped away and percentage viewed, not click-through rate. Since 31 March 2025, every start and replay counts as a public view. Monetization still uses “engaged views”.

Win the first second

Motion and a question on screen at frame 1. Every top Short we pulled has a question or claim title: “Why wasn't Portugal conquered by Spain?”

Keep it short, make it loop

25–40 s. The last line leads back into the first, so replays add views and percentage viewed goes above 100%.

One niche per channel

The test audience is chosen from who watched you before. Mixing topics confuses it. This is why we're launching three channels rather than one.

Recurring series and cast

A named series and returning characters turn one viral viewer into a subscriber. SolarBalls' cast is its moat.

Post every day

Each upload is a fresh test. Volume raises the hit rate, as long as quality holds.

Ride the calendar

Anniversaries, news hooks and data releases. Topic radar flags them a week ahead.

Cross-post the master

The same clean file (no watermark) goes to TikTok, Instagram Reels and Facebook Reels. More reach for no extra production.

Learn from each upload

At 48 hours, pull the swipe rate and percentage viewed; the best hooks feed the next topic scores. Baselines get set after the first 10 uploads per channel.

What we won't do: paid promotion, engagement bait, re-uploading other creators' work, or template spam. YouTube's “inauthentic content” rule (15 July 2025) demonetizes mass-produced, low-substance uploads. The fact gate and a real hook in every episode keep us on the right side of it.
