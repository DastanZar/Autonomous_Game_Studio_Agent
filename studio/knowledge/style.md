# House style (all channels)

The narrator is dry. The facts are absurd. The gap between the two is the joke.

## Sentences
- One idea per sentence, one sentence per line where you can. 6–14 words is the sweet spot.
- Put the concrete noun first: "The army sent one major." not "What the army decided to do was send…"
- State; don't sell. Never "incredible", "insane", "mind-blowing". If it's incredible, the fact shows it.
- Contrast carries the joke: the expectation in one line, the reality in the next, a gap between them.
  "So they mounted a gun on a truck." (0.2 s) "The emus were faster than the truck."
- The rule of three, then break it: "one major, two soldiers, two machine guns, and ten thousand bullets."

## Numbers
- Spell them as spoken. Round in speech ("nearly ten thousand"), exact in captions ("9,860") if sourced.
- One hard number per episode is the one people repeat. Make it land on its own line ("Ten bullets per bird.").

## Structure (Shorts)
- 0–2 s: the hook sentence. No logo, no greeting, no "so".
- Every 3–5 s: a new beat and a new picture.
- Last 3 s: the callback, short enough to land before the viewer swipes.
- Loop: the last line should make the first frame feel like the next line.

## Voice and cast
- Narrator lines: past tense, third person, deadpan.
- Cast lines (character channels): the character's trait from the bible in every line, but the fact
  in the line must still be sourced. Characters exaggerate their feelings, never the facts.

## Never
- Banned phrases in the bible, clickbait questions without answers, "fun fact", calls to subscribe
  in the narration, medical or financial advice, mocking victims of real tragedies.
